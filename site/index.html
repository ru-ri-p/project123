The problem

AI moves fast. Proof doesn't keep up.

When a regulator asks what your AI told a client six months ago — and whether anyone changed the record since — an ordinary log is not an answer. Logs can be edited, and a log you could edit is a log a regulator can't rely on. In DIFC and ADGM, where record-keeping is a licence condition, that gap is a real exposure.

How it works

One check, on the way out the door

Your product makes one call before an AI output reaches a user. Everything else — evaluation, the fix, the sealed record — happens inside it.

01

Check

The output is judged against your own policy and the rulebooks that apply to you, and landed on a green–red risk scale.

02

Fix

If something's wrong, Attest proposes a fix — a redaction, or a model rewrite it has re-checked as compliant before offering.

03

Decide

You choose per risk level: let routine fixes apply automatically, and send the serious ones to a named person for clearance.

04

Seal

The decision and the output are signed, chained, and anchored to an independent timestamp — provable later, unforgeable after.

“We flagged it. Here is the fix. Here is proof the fix shipped. And nobody can doctor that history.”

The sentence Attest is built to let you say — to a regulator, an auditor, or a board — about any AI action your firm has taken.

Why it's different

The proof survives a bad actor — including us

01

Anchored beyond reach

Records are timestamped against an external authority, so history can't be rewritten after the fact — not by an attacker, an insider, or Attest itself.

02

Your keys, your content

In customer-key mode your content is encrypted to a key you hold. Stored records stay dark to Attest and its hosting until you release one.

03

Only your policy blocks

Jurisdiction rulebooks are advisory — they cite and raise risk, but only your own reviewed policy can ever stop your business.

04

Verifiable by outsiders

Every case exports as a bundle with a standalone script a regulator runs on their own machine — no access to your systems required.

05

Named accountability

When a person approves a sensitive output, the sealed record names that verified individual — the evidence a record-keeping regime asks for.

06

One line to integrate

A single gate call in your serving path, an SDK that depends on almost nothing, and a console your compliance team actually works in.
