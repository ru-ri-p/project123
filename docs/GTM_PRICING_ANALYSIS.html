The one-line market thesis. AI is entering regulated financial workflows faster than the ability to prove what it did. In the DIFC and ADGM, where record-keeping and data protection are licence conditions, that gap is a live compliance exposure — and the firms feeling it first are the ones putting AI in front of clients today. Attest sells the proof, not the AI.

01 · Ideal customer

Who this is for

The sharpest fit is an organisation that (a) uses AI in a workflow a regulator cares about, (b) operates under a record-keeping or data-protection regime, and (c) has someone whose job is personally on the line if an AI decision can't be accounted for. Three concentric rings:

Ring 1
Regulated fintech & market-intelligence in the DIFC/ADGM putting AI outputs in front of clients — the beachhead. Fast-moving, feel the pain now, short sales cycle. (e.g. the pilot customer.)
Ring 2
Banks, wealth & asset managers with AI innovation programmes and formal compliance functions. Larger contracts, longer cycles, procurement and security review.
Ring 3
Government & public bodies adopting AI who need a defensible, independently-verifiable trail of every automated decision. Slowest, largest, most durable.

Qualifying signals (fits) and anti-signals (skip)

  • Fits: AI touches client-facing or decision-making output; a named CCO, MLRO or Head of Compliance; a DIFC/ADGM licence; a public AI launch in the last year; a board that has asked "how do we govern this?"
  • Skip for now: pure back-office AI with no external exposure; no compliance function; unregulated; wants Attest to decide legality (Attest proves records, it does not certify lawfulness).

02 · The people

Who signs, who champions, who blocks

Economic buyer

Chief Compliance Officer / MLRO

Owns the regulatory risk. Buys because an unprovable AI record is their exposure at the next audit. Speaks "record-keeping obligation," not "hash chain."

Champion

Head of AI / Innovation / Product

Wants to ship AI features without compliance saying no. Attest is the thing that lets the launch happen. Often finds you first.

Technical gate

CISO / Head of Security

Runs the security review. Won over by customer-key mode, the minimal dependency surface, external anchoring, and SECURITY.md. Can veto.

Approver

General Counsel / Risk Committee

Signs off on the "provable to a regulator" claim and the pilot terms. Cares that Attest is honest about what it does and doesn't certify.

03 · The value

What they're actually buying

Not a log — an insurance-grade, independently-verifiable record that converts an unbounded, hard-to-quantify regulatory risk into a bounded, demonstrable control. Four value lines, roughly in the order buyers feel them:

ValueWhat it replacesWhy it's worth paying for
Regulatory risk reductionAn editable log a regulator can't rely onEnforcement, fines and licence risk in the DIFC/ADGM are existential for a small firm; a provable trail is cheap against that tail.
Faster, safer AI launchCompliance blocking the roadmapGovernance that unblocks a launch has direct revenue value — the champion's real motivation.
Lower audit & evidence costManual evidence-gathering under deadlineOne-click, self-verifying evidence bundles turn days of scramble into minutes.
Trust as sales enablement"Trust us" to their own clientsThe customer can now prove their AI is governed — a differentiator they resell.
The framing that lands: "We flagged it, here's the fix, here's proof the fix shipped, and nobody can doctor that history." Attest is a speed camera, not the speed limit — it proves records are unaltered; it does not certify lawfulness. Being honest about that boundary is itself part of the sale to counsel.

04 · Why not the alternatives

The wedge

AlternativeWhere it falls short
Build it in-house / app logsA log the firm can edit proves nothing to a regulator. No external anchor, no offline verifier, and it's now a system they must maintain and defend.
Generic AI-governance / eval toolsMeasure model quality and bias; they don't produce a tamper-evident, externally-anchored, independently-verifiable record of each action.
GRC / audit platformsTrack policies and controls at the org level, not a signed, per-action chain of what the AI actually did.
Cloud audit trailsTrust the same vendor that stores them; Attest anchors externally so not even Attest can rewrite history, and customer-key keeps content dark to the vendor.

Three things are hard to copy and worth leading with: external anchoring (defends against insiders on both sides, including us), customer-key confidentiality (content dark to the vendor), and offline-verifiable evidence (a regulator checks it with no access to your systems).

05 · Pricing model

How to charge, by segment

The recommended structure is a platform fee + usage hybrid. The platform fee captures the value of the compliance surface (policy engine, console, evidence, roles); usage — priced per gated action — aligns cost with the value delivered and scales naturally as the customer puts more AI through Attest. Per-gated-action is the right metric: it's the unit of value (one provable AI decision), it's transparent, and it grows with the account without punishing early adoption.

How to get to real numbers. Treat the ranges above as hypotheses. Validate with 3–5 pilot conversations: ask what an unprovable AI record would cost them at audit, what they budget for compliance tooling, and what a launch delay costs. Price against the risk avoided, not the compute consumed — the value is insurance, and insurance isn't priced by the kilobyte.

06 · The motion

Land, expand, deepen

  • Land — a scoped, paid or design-partner pilot on one AI workflow (as with the current pilot). Prove the loop and the evidence bundle on synthetic data.
  • Expand — more actions, more seats, more workflows under the same platform fee as their AI usage grows.
  • Deepen — customer-key, counsel-reviewed packs, region/on-prem, SLA: the add-ons that raise both price and switching cost.

Early on, prefer a small number of reference design-partners over revenue maximisation. A named DIFC-regulated firm that will vouch for the evidence bundle is worth more than its contract — it's the proof the next ten buyers need.

07 · Honest caveats

What could be wrong here

  • Prices are a model, not a market read. No willingness-to-pay data yet; validate before quoting.
  • Packs are pre-counsel. The premium content line depends on legal review that hasn't happened; today's packs are advisory and labelled as such.
  • The buyer may be created, not found. Some targets don't yet know they need provable AI records — that lengthens the cycle and raises the education cost.
  • Concentration risk. A single-jurisdiction beachhead (UAE) is the right focus, but plan the second market before over-indexing.
